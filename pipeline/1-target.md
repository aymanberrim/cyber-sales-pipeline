# 1 · Target

**Input:** the vendor's product reality (what it attacks and hardens).
**Output:** a named account universe, ranked into the three rings, with a trigger on each account.
**Exit criterion:** every account on the list runs — or is about to run — at least one AI agent with real authority (tools, data, or money).
**Metric:** % of the list with a dated buying signal (target: 100% — no signal, no account).

## The ICP, stated as a rule

A target qualifies when **an AI agent in its product or operations can take an action that matters** — call a tool, touch customer data, move money, act without a human in the loop. That is the attack surface the vendor exists for. A company "doing AI" with a read-only chatbot and no tool access is out of scope until it ships autonomy.

## The three rings (work them warm → cold)

- **Ring 1 — Accelerator / investor portfolio.** Warm, when the vendor is accelerator- or fund-backed. The founder is inside the community, so an intro path exists before the message does. Smallest ring, start here to get the first proof points and testimonials.
- **Ring 2 — Recently-funded startup batches.** Cold but signal-guaranteed: fund-fresh, agent-native, founder-led, fast to sign a red-team. The volume engine.
- **Ring 3 — Large enterprises**, home market first (manufacturing, finance, robotics/mobility, infrastructure). Big tickets, long cycles. Enter later, with Ring 1/2 proof in hand.

## Triggers (the only reason to touch an account)

Just shipped or announced an agent feature · raised a round · hiring AI/agent/LLM engineers · published an engineering post on their agent architecture · a provider case study names them · a public incident or near-miss in their space. One trigger is enough; none means the account waits.

## Handoff

Pass each qualified account to **2 · Source** with its ring, its trigger and a first guess at its agent stack to confirm.
