# 4 · Engage

**Input:** segmented groups, each with a lead vulnerability and one message angle.
**Output:** a running sequence that earns replies, backed by a public proof artifact.
**Exit criterion:** sequence live for every group; proof artifact published; sending infrastructure warmed.
**Metric:** open rate and reply rate per group (pause triggers: open < 35%, reply < 3%).

## Proof before pitch

The engagement opens with a **resource magnet**, not a cold ask. The founder publishes a short, concrete artifact — *"we red-teamed 5 open-source agent setups; here are the 3 things that break first."* It does the credibility work; the outbound simply points to it and to the target's own exposure. This fits how the founder already operates (talks, hackathons, community) and keeps the vendor on the proof side of a noisy "AI security" market.

## The sequence (shape)

1. **Invitation** — ultra-short, references *their* agent, offers the finding. No pitch.
2. **On accept — the value** — their specific exposure: "on a `<stack>` setup, this is the injection that lands," measurable, not a brochure.
3. **The CTA** — one only: a scoped, free red-team of one agent, or "20 min, I show you one live attack on an agent like yours."
4. **Follow-up** — short, oblique ("by the way, I'll be running agent-attack demos next week…").

Full copy and variants: [`messages/sequence.md`](../messages/sequence.md).

## Capture & enrichment (spend money late)

Email in bulk; the phone number only once a lead is in ICP and has replied, clicked or tested. No inferred emails.

## Handoff

A reply moves the lead to **5 · Convert**. Silence routes it back through the follow-up track, then to nurture.
